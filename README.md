# CRA Article 14 Reporting Kit: Lite

A free decision guide and a 24-hour early-warning template for manufacturers of connected products that must report to ENISA under the EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847).

Article 14 reporting has applied since **11 September 2026**. If a vulnerability in your product is being actively exploited, or a severe incident affects it, the clock starts the moment you become aware: **24 hours** for the early warning, **72 hours** for the detailed notification.

## What is inside

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `CRA-Article14-Reporting-Kit-Lite.pdf` | 4-page guide: the reporting clock, a four-question "do we have to report this?" decision guide, which coordinating CSIRT to select, and a set-up checklist for ENISA's Single Reporting Platform |
| `CRA-Article14-Early-Warning-Template.docx` | Editable Word template: early-warning content, a four-timestamp clock log, deadline table and sign-off |

## Who this is for

Manufacturers (hardware or software) of products with digital elements sold in the EU, including products already on the market. Product security leads, compliance officers and small teams that do not yet have a runbook for the first 24 hours.

## The reporting clock at a glance

| Stage | Actively exploited vulnerability | Severe incident |
|---|---|---|
| Early warning | 24 hours from awareness | 24 hours from awareness |
| Notification | 72 hours from awareness | 72 hours from awareness |
| Final report | 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available | One month after the 72-hour notification |

This kit is a practical aid, **not legal advice**. It reflects ENISA's published FAQ and third-party guides as of 12 November 2026. Rules and platform fields can change: check the Regulation and ENISA's pages before you file.
